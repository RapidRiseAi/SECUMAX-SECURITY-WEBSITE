Division 02

Forensic Services

Evidence is at its most informative before anyone has handled it, and at its most fragile in exactly the same moment. This division collects, examines and reports on physical, documentary and digital material under an unbroken chain of custody.

PSIRA Registered & Compliant Chain of Custody Court-Ready Reporting
What this division does

From the scene to the witness box.

Forensic work begins where an incident stops being a story and starts being evidence. A storeroom that has been forced, a laptop that was wiped over a weekend, a signature that does not sit comfortably beside the others on file — each of these carries information, and each of them degrades the moment it is handled without method. The Forensic Services division exists to capture that information properly the first time, because there is rarely a second opportunity.

Everything recovered is recorded from the moment of collection. Scenes are photographed and documented before anything is moved, exhibits are sealed, labelled and logged, and digital material is imaged so that examination is carried out on a verified copy rather than on the original device. That record — what was taken, by whom, when, and where it has been since — is the chain of custody. It is what allows a finding to be tested by someone else instead of simply believed.

Examination and reporting are kept deliberately independent of the outcome a client would prefer. Our examiners report what the material supports, what it does not support, and where it is genuinely inconclusive. Reports are written for readers who were not present and who do not share the technical background, and the examiner who did the work remains available to explain the method and the findings in a disciplinary hearing, an arbitration or a court.

What this covers

Crime scene processing & documentation

Systematic examination of a scene before it is disturbed — photography, sketches, measurements and a written record of conditions as found. Exhibits are recovered, sealed and logged on site.

Fingerprint collection & comparison

Development, lifting and preservation of latent prints from surfaces at a scene or from recovered items. Comparison work is documented so that the basis for any identification can be reviewed by another examiner.

Questioned document examination

Examination of signatures, handwriting, alterations, insertions and printing on disputed documents. Frequently required in contract disputes, cheque and cash-book matters, and challenges to wills and agreements.

Digital & mobile device forensics

Forensic imaging and examination of computers, phones and storage media, including deleted or hidden material where it remains recoverable. The original device is preserved and all work is done on a verified image.

Forensic auditing

Reconstruction of financial records to establish how value moved and where the control failed. Structured for use in recovery action, insurance claims and disciplinary proceedings rather than as a routine audit.

Evidence handling & chain of custody

Sealing, labelling, transport, storage and documented transfer of exhibits. Every movement of an item is recorded so that its integrity can be demonstrated rather than asserted.

Expert witness testimony

Our examiners attend hearings, arbitrations and court to present their findings and to explain the methods behind them, including under cross-examination by the other side.

Court-ready forensic reporting

Written reports setting out the material examined, the method applied, the findings and their limitations — in language a decision-maker can follow without specialist knowledge.

How we approach it

Nothing moves until it is recorded.

The sequence below is deliberately rigid. It is the part that makes a finding defensible months later.

01

Secure and assess

The scene, device or document set is isolated so that nothing further is added, removed or altered. We establish what is present, what is at risk of degrading first, and what has already been handled before our arrival.

02

Collect and document

Conditions as found are photographed and written up before anything is moved. Items are recovered, sealed, labelled and logged; digital media is imaged and the image verified against the original.

03

Examine and analyse

Work is carried out on the copy or the recovered exhibit, following an established method, with contemporaneous notes recorded as the examination proceeds so that another examiner could repeat it.

04

Report and testify

Findings are written up with the method, the limitations and the reasoning made explicit. Where the matter proceeds, the examiner who did the work presents and defends those findings in person.

Common questions

What clients usually ask first.

If your question is not answered here, put it to us directly — the answer will be a straight one.

What material can this division actually examine?
Physical scenes, recovered exhibits and latent fingerprints; disputed documents, signatures and handwriting; computers, mobile devices and storage media; and financial records where the question is how value moved rather than whether the books balance. Where a specialist discipline falls outside our capability, we will say so rather than stretch to cover it.
What does chain of custody mean in practice?
It means every exhibit has a continuous, written history: who collected it, when and from where; how it was sealed and stored; and every person who has held it since. If that history has a gap, the other side can argue the item may have been altered. Maintaining it is administrative, unglamorous work, and it is the difference between evidence and an assertion.
The scene has already been cleaned up. Is there any point?
Often there is, though the picture is weaker. Digital records, access-control and CCTV data, documents and financial trails are usually still intact even when a physical scene is not. Tell us what has been moved and by whom — that itself is information, and it lets us set realistic expectations before you commit to anything.
Who sees the report and the material behind it?
You do, and anyone you instruct us to release it to. Exhibits and working files are held securely and are not discussed outside the assigned examination team and the director accountable for the matter. Where a report is destined for a hearing or a court, we will tell you in advance what the other side is likely to be entitled to see.
