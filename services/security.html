Security Services

This is the division that designs the system: what needs protecting, how it is controlled, what is watched, and what happens when something is triggered. We assess the risk first, then build the access control, surveillance and written policy framework that answers it.

The division

Assess first, then design the system.

Most sites do not have a security problem so much as a security design problem. Cameras that cover the wrong ground, a gate register nobody reads, an alarm reporting to a number that changed two years ago, a procedure that exists on paper and nowhere else. Our starting point is therefore an assessment rather than a quotation: what the site actually holds, how someone would realistically reach it, where people, vehicles and stock move, and where the current arrangement stops working.

From that assessment we design the controls in layers — perimeter treatment and physical barriers, access control at the points where people and vehicles enter, camera coverage placed for identification rather than decoration, detection and alarm equipment tied to a defined response, and a control room or monitoring arrangement that a named role is accountable for. Where hardware is involved we specify it, install it and commission it against the assessment, so that every camera and every reader exists for a documented reason and can be checked against that reason later.

Equipment on its own changes very little. What holds a system together is the written framework around it: standard operating procedures for the people on site, escalation routes that name roles rather than individuals, visitor and contractor rules, key and access register control, and a review cycle that confirms the system is still being used as designed. We audit our own installations and other people's. And because our investigation and forensic divisions work from the same footage, registers and access logs, a system built here is built to produce material that still means something in a disciplinary hearing, an insurer's review or a court.

What this covers

Security risk assessments

A structured survey of the site, its assets, its access points and its current arrangement, written up as prioritised vulnerabilities with recommended controls against each.

Access control & visitor management

Controlled entry for staff, contractors, visitors and vehicles, with credentials, registers and permissions that produce a reliable record of who was on site and when.

CCTV design, installation & monitoring

Camera layouts planned around identification, coverage and lighting rather than camera count, then installed, commissioned and monitored against that design.

Alarm systems & response coordination

Detection and alarm equipment tied to a written response plan, so that a signal reaches a person who knows what to do with it and who is accountable for doing it.

Perimeter & physical security design

Fencing, gates, lighting, barriers and building hardening treated as one layered design, aimed at delaying and detecting an approach rather than merely marking a boundary.

Control room & monitoring solutions

Monitoring arrangements with defined operator duties, logging discipline and escalation routes, whether the room sits on your site or the feed is watched remotely.

Security policy & SOP development

Written procedures for the people who actually work the system — patrol and post orders, visitor and contractor rules, key control, incident reporting and escalation.

Security audits & compliance reviews

A measured review of an existing arrangement — ours or another provider's — testing equipment, records and procedures against the design and against PSIRA requirements.

Our method

How we approach it.

Nothing is specified before it has been justified, and nothing is handed over before it has been tested.

01

Site assessment

We walk the site with the people who work it. What it holds, how it is approached, where the perimeter is weak, how staff, contractors, visitors, vehicles and stock actually move, what equipment is already installed and what it genuinely covers. The output is a written assessment with vulnerabilities set out in order of priority.

02

Design & specification

Controls are designed in layers against the assessment — deter, delay, detect, respond — and written up as a specification detailed enough to be compared against alternatives. Every element is traceable to a vulnerability, so you can see why it is on the list and what happens if it is left off.

03

Installation & commissioning

Equipment is installed, configured and then tested against the design rather than against a datasheet: camera views checked for identification, readers checked against the access matrix, detection checked for coverage. Handover includes as-built documentation and training for the people who will use it daily.

04

Policy, audit & review

Procedures, registers and escalation routes are written so the system has an operating manual, not just hardware. We then audit against it on an agreed cycle, and re-test whenever the site, the layout or the risk changes — because a system that is not reviewed quietly stops matching the site it was built for.

Before you commission work

Common questions.

What does a security risk assessment actually cover?
Site layout and perimeter, every point where people and vehicles enter and leave, the movement of staff, contractors, visitors and stock, the equipment already installed and what it genuinely covers, the procedures as written against the procedures as practised, and any previous incidents you are willing to share. You receive a written assessment that names the vulnerabilities, puts them in order of priority and sets a recommended control against each one.
Do we have to use your equipment or your officers?
No. The assessment is a deliverable in its own right and it belongs to you. We will specify, install and monitor where you want us to, and we will work alongside an existing installation or an existing provider where you do not. If the arrangement you already have is adequate, we will say so rather than recommending replacement for its own sake.
Who sees the assessment, and how is our information handled?
An assessment describes precisely how someone could get into your site, so it is treated accordingly. It is confidential, released only to the client contacts you nominate, and never used as a case study or a reference. Site drawings, camera layouts, access matrices and register data stay inside the engagement, and our officers are vetted and registered under the PSIRA framework.
What happens to footage and access records after an incident?
The systems we design are meant to produce material that is still usable months later, so footage, alarm events and access logs are exported and handled under a documented chain of custody rather than copied ad hoc onto somebody's device. That matters when the same material has to be put in front of a disciplinary hearing, an insurer or a court — and it lets our investigation and forensic divisions work from the record the system was built to create.
