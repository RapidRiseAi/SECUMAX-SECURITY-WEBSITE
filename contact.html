Common questions

Before you write to us.

What happens after I make contact?

The first exchange is a confidential consultation. You describe what has happened or what you are worried about, and we establish the facts already in your possession and the gaps that matter. Nothing is committed to at that stage. If we are not the right people for the matter, we say so then rather than later.

Is my enquiry confidential?

Yes. Discretion is the default position, not an upgrade. Enquiries, files and client identities are not discussed outside the engagement, and that holds whether or not the enquiry becomes a mandate. Where an investigation is internal, we work to keep the fact of it contained as well as its findings.

What information should I have ready?

A short account of what happened and when, who is involved or affected, and what you would like the outcome to be. If documents, records, images or messages exist, say that they exist — do not attach anything sensitive to a first email. Preserve the original material and change nothing about it: original files, records and devices are far more useful to a forensic examination than copies, and preserving them protects the chain of custody from the outset.

Which division handles what?

Investigation Services handles enquiry into fraud, misconduct and loss. Forensic Services handles scene work, examination and court-ready reporting. Polygraph Services handles structured credibility assessment. Security Services covers risk assessment, systems and policy; Protection Services covers close protection of people; Guarding Services covers manned site security; and Specialized Services covers accredited firearm training, escorts, recovery and bespoke work. If you are not sure which applies, select “Not sure yet” on the form and describe the situation — routing it correctly is our job, not yours.

Tell us what happened.

Every enquiry is confidential. You will get an honest answer about whether we can help — and what it would actually involve.
