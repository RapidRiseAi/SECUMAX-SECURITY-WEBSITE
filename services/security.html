Security Services

This is the division that puts a planned security presence around something valuable, a site, an operation, a consignment or a single movement. What deploys, and how much of it, is scoped to the value of the asset, the route it has to travel and the threat environment around it.

PSIRA Registered 24/7 Operations National Coverage
The division

Security scoped to the asset and the route.

Operational security starts with something that has a value: a consignment, a stockpile, a payload, an operation running under pressure. What that thing is worth to the person who wants to take it, and where it sits or where it has to go, is what decides the security response. Our starting point is therefore the asset and the risk attached to it rather than a standard package: what is being protected, who already knows about it, what the environment around it looks like, and what would realistically have to happen for it to be lost.

A movement is a different problem from a fixed position. Exposure is not spread evenly across it: it concentrates at loading and offloading, at predictable points on a route, at handovers, and at any delay nobody planned for. Bullion runs and other high-value movements are therefore planned before they are staffed: route and alternatives, timing, loading and handover points, communications, who needs to know and who does not, and what the team does if the plan has to change while the movement is under way.

Not every requirement is a movement. A risk-based security presence can just as easily be scoped to a site, an operation or a single assignment, for as long as the risk lasts and at a level set against that risk rather than against a standard rate. Because INTEGRI runs investigation and protection capability alongside this division, a requirement and the enquiry behind it can sit with one team: where something has already been taken, investigators work the same matter; where the exposure is a person rather than an asset, the protection division carries it. Every assignment is closed out in writing: what was deployed, what happened, and anything that should change next time.

Our method

How we approach it.

Nothing deploys before it has been planned, and no assignment closes without a written record of what happened.

01

Requirement & risk

We start with what is being protected and what it is worth to someone who wants to take it. Where it sits or where it has to go, who already knows about it, what the environment around it looks like, and what has gone wrong before. The output is a risk picture the deployment can be measured against, and an honest answer if the requirement is smaller than you expected.

02

Operational planning

The assignment is planned before it is staffed. Where there is a movement: route and alternatives, timing, loading and handover points, communications and contingencies. Where there is a fixed position: the hours that matter, the points that carry the exposure and what the team does when something changes. You see the plan and agree it before anyone deploys.

03

Deployment

Officers deploy to that plan: briefed on the asset, the route and the contingencies rather than posted to a generic assignment. Our officers are registered and vetted within the PSIRA framework, and one point of contact stays yours for the duration, so there is always someone accountable who knows the detail.

04

Debrief & written record

Every assignment is closed out in writing: what was deployed, what happened, anything that departed from the plan and anything that should change next time. Where something was lost or taken, that record is the starting point for our investigation division rather than an enquiry beginning from nothing.

Before you commission work

Common questions.

What does “risk-based” actually mean in practice?
That the level of cover is set by the risk in front of us rather than by a standard package. We look at what is being protected and what it is worth to someone who wants it, where it sits or where it has to travel, who already knows about it, and what has gone wrong before. The deployment is then scoped to that picture, and if the honest answer is that the requirement is smaller than you expected, we will say so rather than quote for more than you need.
Do you protect assets on the move as well as in one place?
Yes. Bullion runs and other high-value movements are a named part of this division, alongside protective services for valuable goods and assets being moved between locations. A movement is planned as a movement: route and alternatives, timing, loading and handover points, communications and contingencies, all agreed before the team deploys rather than decided on the day.
How is information about a movement handled?
On a need-to-know basis, because the detail of a high-value movement is exactly what makes it vulnerable. Routes, timings, quantities and the identity of the client stay inside the assignment, are held only by the people who have to hold them, and are never used as a case study or a reference. Our officers are vetted and registered under the PSIRA framework.
What happens if something goes wrong on an assignment?
The assignment is closed out in writing regardless of outcome: what was deployed, what happened, and anything that departed from the plan. Where something was lost or taken, that written record is where our investigation division starts, which is a great deal more useful than an enquiry reconstructed weeks later. It is written to be read by someone who was not there: an insurer, an internal enquiry or a court.
