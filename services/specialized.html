The division

Mining security, and the work that sits around it.

A mine is not an ordinary site. High-value commodities move through it, large numbers of people move with them, the infrastructure is spread out and difficult to overlook, and illegal mining activity brings a category of risk that a conventional security brief was never written for. Periods of heightened operational risk — a shutdown, a shift change, a dispute, a spike in illegal activity — put pressure on all of it at once. Mining security is this division's central capability, and it is built around those realities rather than adapted to them afterwards.

Around it sit the functions a mining operation actually asks for: specialist illegal mining prevention teams, operational support during protests, labour unrest and other high-risk public-order situations, dedicated searches carried out according to the needs of the operation or the investigation, and mining investigations into theft, illegal activity, internal misconduct and other mining-related security incidents. Each is scoped in writing, and each is designed to work with the mine's own management, security function and procedures rather than around them.

Everything here is bounded by law and by that written scope. Public-order support is defensive and de-escalation-first, provided to protect a client's people, premises and access — never to interfere with anyone's lawful right to gather or to withhold their labour, and never as a substitute for the police. Illegal mining work is prevention, detection and lawful response, coordinated with the mine and with the authorities, not a force acting on its own initiative. Searches are carried out on the client's mandate, in line with the site's own search policy and the rights of the people involved. Where officers are deployed armed they are registered for that role and operate within the applicable firearm and private security regulations. If a brief cannot be carried out lawfully and proportionately, we say so and decline it.

The division also carries INTEGRI's training offering: firearm training focused on responsible, competent and safe firearm handling; corporate training in security awareness adapted to a corporate environment; riot control training that prepares personnel for protests, riots and civil unrest; and security training covering awareness, procedures and practical security responsibilities. On firearm training we prepare and assess candidates properly — the licence decision rests with the Registrar, not with us, and we will never suggest otherwise.

Our method

How we approach it.

Bespoke work needs tighter definition than routine work, not looser.

01

Define the requirement

We start with the actual problem rather than the service you think you need — the site, the commodity, the people affected, the pattern of loss or unrest — and establish what a good outcome looks like and what constraints you are working within.

02

Lawful & proportionate scoping

We establish what can be done lawfully and proportionately, what it exposes you to, and where the boundaries sit — including, for search, public-order and illegal mining work, the client's mandate, the site's own policies and the rights of the people involved. Anything falling outside those boundaries is declined at this stage rather than halfway through.

03

Written brief & team assignment

The operation is set out in writing — objective, method, roles, reporting lines, escalation and contingencies — and staffed from the divisions the work genuinely requires, not from whoever is available.

04

Execution & documented handover

The work is carried out to the brief and recorded as it goes, so that what you receive at the end is a documented account you can act on, place before a hearing or hand to an insurer.

Before you call

Common questions.

What does your firearm training actually cover?
Firearm training with us is focused on responsible, competent and safe firearm handling: the legal framework, safe storage, sound handling and practical judgement about when a firearm is and is not the answer. We prepare and assess candidates. We do not issue licences and we cannot promise one — that decision belongs to the Registrar, and any provider who suggests otherwise should be treated with caution.
What does riot or civil unrest control involve — and what does it not?
It is lawful, defensive operational support to a client's site and personnel during protests, labour unrest, riots and other high-risk public-order situations. The posture is de-escalation first: protect people, protect access, protect property, record what happens, and keep a situation from escalating. It is not strike-breaking, it is not an offensive capability, and it is not a substitute for the police — where a matter belongs with the SAPS, that is where it goes. A brief that asks us to interfere with a lawful gathering or a lawful withdrawal of labour is declined. Riot control training teaches the same discipline: lawful conduct, restraint, de-escalation and the limits of a private security role.
How do illegal mining prevention teams operate?
As prevention, detection and lawful response — coordinated with the mine and with the authorities, never as a force acting on its own initiative. The teams work to a written scope agreed with mine management, report through a defined line, and hand criminal matters to the police rather than dealing with them privately. Where an incident needs to be taken further, our mining investigations capability documents it to a standard that holds up afterwards.
On what basis are dedicated searches carried out?
Only on the client's mandate, and only within the site's own search policy and the rights of the people being searched. What is being searched, why, by whom, on what consent framework and how the result is recorded are all agreed in writing before a search happens. If a proposed search cannot be conducted lawfully and with proper regard for employees' rights, we say so rather than carry it out.
How do you decide whether to take on a bespoke brief?
We scope it before we quote on it. If the objective can be achieved lawfully and proportionately, we set out the method, the roles and the reporting in writing and work to that scope. If it cannot, we say so and decline the work — an operation that cannot be defended afterwards is of no use to a client, however well it appears to have gone at the time.
Who carries out this work, and how are they vetted?
Personnel and instructors are drawn from our own divisions, are registered with PSIRA where the role requires it, and are screened before assignment — identity verification, criminal record checks and references, with polygraph examination through our own examiners where the sensitivity of the assignment warrants it. Every deployment has a named supervisor with a defined reporting line, and reporting goes to one named contact on your side. We do not discuss clients or assignments with anyone else.
